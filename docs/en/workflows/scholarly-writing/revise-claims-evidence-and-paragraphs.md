# How do I revise claims, evidence, and paragraphs?

<div class="answer-meta" markdown>
<span>Scholarly writing</span><span>beginner</span><span>60–120 min</span>
</div>

## What you are trying to do

How can revision test whether a paper's argument is warranted rather than merely make its sentences smoother? Separate passes prevent proofreading from concealing a weak claim, missing transition, inaccurate quotation, or contradictory chart.

Revision is an audit trail. A tracked change records a textual proposal; a comment records a question or rationale; a revision log records the consequential decision and its evidence. None should be accepted automatically, whether it came from a peer, an instructor, a grammar tool, or a generative AI system.

## You need

- a backed-up draft with its argument plan and source-to-claim table;
- access to every quoted or closely paraphrased source;
- all tables and figures with their source data and checks;
- comments and tracked changes from reviewers, if any;
- the [sample ZIP](../../../assets/downloads/scholarly-work-foundations-v1.zip) for a known-problem paragraph, revised paragraph, and revision log.

## Workflow

1. **Create a revision log.** Record date, pass, location, problem, evidence checked, decision, and unresolved consequence. Give important changes identifiers such as `R-01`. Do not log every comma; log decisions another reader would need to understand the argument's development.

2. **Substantive pass: test the central claim.** Restate the research question and final claim without looking at the introduction. Ask what evidence would make the claim false, what alternative interpretation remains plausible, and whether the limitation is strong enough. Narrow or qualify the claim before polishing it.

3. **Evidence pass: trace every support.** Mark unsupported claims, vague references such as “research shows,” and sources used only by reputation. For every quotation, compare words, ellipses, emphasis, spelling, and locator with the source. For every paraphrase, verify meaning and citation. An exact quotation with the wrong page is still a failed check.

4. **Find orphan quotations.** For each quotation or table reference, identify the claim it supports and the reasoning after it. Integrate, relocate, paraphrase, or remove material that the paper never interprets. Evidence does not explain its own significance.

5. **Structural pass: map the sections again.** Write each section's function in the margin. Identify repeated points, background that arrives too late, conclusions introduced before their evidence, and missing steps between claims. Move whole sections before sentence-level editing.

6. **Paragraph pass: inspect the local move.** Underline the paragraph's claim, bracket its evidence, and circle the reasoning and transition. Split a paragraph that performs unrelated moves; combine fragments that repeat one move. Replace a transition such as “Furthermore” with the actual relation—contrast, cause, qualification, scale, or return.

7. **Table and figure pass: audit against prose.** Recalculate or manually check a small subset. Confirm source, unit, denominator, filters, missing values, and transformations. Compare the number, category order, and stated trend with every prose claim. If a figure shows counts, do not write about proportions unless you calculated the denominator.

8. **Review comments and tracked changes.** Read each suggestion with surrounding evidence. Accept, reject, or revise it and record the reason for consequential choices. Keep unresolved comments visible or transfer them to the revision log. Do not accept everything to make the markup disappear.

9. **AI-assistance pass.** Record the tool, version or access date, task, material supplied, and what text or decision it affected. Verify every factual statement, quotation, locator, citation, calculation, and interpretation against the original source or data. Remove sensitive or restricted material from systems not approved to process it. An AI-use statement does not convert unverified output into evidence.

10. **Sentence pass.** Only after the argument and structure are stable, revise subjects and verbs, ambiguous pronouns, excessive nominalization, unexplained terminology, sentence rhythm, and unnecessary repetition. Preserve necessary qualifications rather than deleting them for apparent confidence.

11. **Proofreading pass.** Check spelling, punctuation, names, titles, dates, numbering, citations, bibliography, cross-references, captions, contents, headers, and page breaks in the final format. Proofread once on screen and once in the exported form or a different view. Update fields before the final read.

12. **Close the log.** Mark each entry resolved, accepted as a known limitation, or assigned for follow-up. Summarize the three changes that most affected the interpretation and confirm that the abstract and conclusion match the revised claim.

## Output

Produce a revised document, a revision log, and an AI-use statement. The log must include at least one substantive, structural, evidence, paragraph, figure/table, and proofreading decision.

The workflow passes when every quotation and locator matches its source, each central claim has visible evidence and reasoning, table/figure claims reconcile with checked data, and unresolved matters are named rather than hidden.

## Check yourself

- Which sentence states the final claim, and what evidence could challenge it?
- Does every quotation have a verified locator and your reasoning nearby?
- Are any points repeated because two sections perform the same function?
- Do prose, table, figure, source data, unit, and denominator agree?
- Can you explain why you accepted or rejected each consequential review suggestion?
- Is AI assistance disclosed at the level needed to reproduce and verify its effect?

## Common traps

- Proofreading before deciding whether the argument works.
- Treating the presence of a citation as proof that a claim is supported.
- Accepting all tracked changes or AI suggestions without checking them.
- Revising a chart's appearance while leaving its denominator unclear.
- Deleting qualifications merely to make prose sound decisive.
- Hiding unresolved source or platform uncertainty before submission.
