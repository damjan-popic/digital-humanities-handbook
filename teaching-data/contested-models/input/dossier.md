# Synthetic dossier: source-readable test records

Every statement below is invented for teaching. Dates, names, statuses, language
events and district boundaries do not document historical residents or institutions.
The research situation continues the archival-friction packet's question about
how documentary labels become authoritative. IDs A–F below abbreviate SYN-A–SYN-F.

## SYN-D1 — invented municipal list, 1910-06-01

The list names Anna Kovatsch (A), Bojan Vidmar (B) and Ciril Novak (C).
Its residence entry assigns A to the invented district SYN-EAST. Its occupation
entry calls A a servant (Dienstbotin) for 1910-01-01 through 1910-12-31.
Its institutional language category is German. This is an administrative
classification, not a language test or a record of a conversation.

## SYN-D2 — invented letter, 1910-06-15

Ana Kovač (A) writes to B in Slovene and describes herself as a seamstress
(šivilja) during 1910. She claims to read German. A dated Slovene letter
supports language use on that occasion, not exclusive language knowledge.
The letter says she works near St. Peter; the place is unresolved between
SYN-L1 and SYN-L2. It does not say she moved from her registered residence.

## Names, editorial time and boundaries

A's administrative form Anna Kovatsch is attested by SYN-D1; her signature
Ana Kovač by SYN-D2. Neither is declared the person's timeless preferred name.
An editor first transcribes the signature as Ana Kovać on 2026-09-01, then
supersedes that assertion with Ana Kovač on 2026-09-02. Both states are retained.
The two occupational descriptions remain parallel, not superseded: their
different institutional contexts may explain an apparent contradiction.

For a spatial stress test only, a square territory 0–1000 m in each direction
is divided at x=500 before 1920 and x=600 from 1920. SYN-W is west and SYN-EAST
east. A's residence is stipulated to stay fixed at (550,500) through 1925;
it changes membership without movement.
These are local engineering coordinates, NOT EPSG:3794 and NOT traced from
the authentic Ljubljana map. SYN-L1=(550,500) and SYN-L2=(800,500) are rival
candidates for the invented St. Peter mention. Their labels are teaching
devices, not a historical gazetteer. The authentic map's printed St. Peter
district is inspected separately; shared wording does not identify the fictional
workplace. Dates below use half-open intervals [start,end).

## SYN-NAMES — invented multilingual gazetteer note

The fictional gazetteer uses German St. Peter for both L1 and L2 throughout
1910–1929. For L1 it uses Slovene Šent Peter in 1910–1919 and Šentpeter in
1920–1929; for L2 it uses Sveti Peter throughout. These spellings and validity
dates are stipulated test records, not claims about historical naming practice.
They do not resolve which place the workplace mention identifies.
